A humming outdoor unit with a fan that will not turn is the call we get most in July in Olathe, KS, and the cause is usually a failed run capacitor. That inexpensive component starts the compressor and fan, fades for years without complaint, and then quits under peak load. It is far from the only suspect, though. Contactors pit and stop passing clean power, condenser fan bearings dry out, small refrigerant leaks drain away cooling capacity, and evaporator coils ice over when a clogged filter strangles airflow.

At Olathe HVAC, every AC repair starts with measurement. Capacitors are metered against the rating on the label. Amp draws on the compressor and fan motor are compared to the data plate. Refrigerant charge is judged by superheat and subcooling, not by pressure readings alone, and any icing prompts a look at the evaporator coil, blower wheel, and filter. This matters because a single warm-air symptom can hide four different faults at four very different prices, and a refrigerant top-off that ignores the leak is just a short-term rental on cold air.

Straight answers are part of every visit. If an aging system's compressor has failed, we explain the repair-versus-replacement math plainly and let you decide. Most of the time, though, the fix is simpler, and because our trucks stock capacitors, contactors, fan motors, hard-start kits, and condensate pumps, the house is usually cooling again before we leave. Why Hire a Professional for AC Repair?

Start with the refrigerant. It lives in a sealed, pressurized loop, venting it is prohibited under federal rules, and correct service requires gauges, a vacuum pump, and a recovery machine rather than a store-bought recharge kit. Add capacitors that hold a dangerous charge with the power off and a condensing unit fed by 240 volts, and the risks add up quickly. Then there are the diagnostic traps: an iced coil often points to poor airflow rather than low charge, and topping off a system with an unrepaired leak lets moisture and acid slowly damage the compressor while your money escapes with the refrigerant.

Professional repair gets the whole picture. Our experienced technicians meter electrical components instead of swapping them, weigh charge decisions against measured superheat and subcooling, find leaks with detectors and dye rather than assumptions, and check the filter, blower, and duct static pressure whenever airflow is suspect. That is the difference between a repair that holds through the season and a parts-cannon fix that calls you back in three weeks. And when the system genuinely is not worth saving, we tell you that plainly too, with numbers instead of pressure.

Maintenance Tips for AC Repair

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Book a professional tune-up each spring before the first heat wave finds the weak parts
  • Shade is fine, but do not box in the outdoor unit with fences or covers
  • A new buzz or click at startup often means a weak capacitor, so call before it fails
  • Avoid shutting registers in unused rooms, which raises static pressure and stresses the blower
  • Rinse the outdoor condenser coil each spring and keep vegetation two feet back
  • Flush the condensate drain line with the season's first use to head off clogs
  • Change the filter monthly during cooling season; starved airflow is how coils freeze
