No Surprises

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Nobody likes surprises on the final bill. So Olathe HVAC prices every Olathe job upfront, after a full diagnostic that looks at the equipment's age, fuel type, and tonnage, refrigerant levels, coil and heat exchanger condition, and the ductwork feeding each room. You'll know the problem, what it takes to fix, and what it costs before you say yes.

Most HVAC failures send small signals well before the system stops. Watch for rising utility bills, airflow that feels weaker than it used to, frost on the copper lines, or rooms that never catch up to the thermostat. Behind those symptoms are usually pinhole refrigerant leaks, worn blower bearings, or a capacitor on its way out. An early service call at the first sign usually costs far less than a failed compressor.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Many Olathe homeowners call us to evaluate an aging system before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical AC or furnace lifespan of 12 to 18 years, a replacement on your own timeline beats scrambling for a new unit during a cold snap. We'll give you an honest read on how much life your equipment has left.

Seasonal Tune-Ups

Maintenance Plans for Olathe Homes

A Olathe HVAC maintenance visit is far more than a quick filter swap. The technician cleans the coils, verifies the refrigerant charge, tests capacitors and contactors, inspects the burners and heat exchanger, clears the condensate drain, replaces the filter, and measures static pressure across the ducts. Minor faults are caught while they're still cheap to fix, and your system keeps running clean and efficient.

No two maintenance plans look exactly alike. A furnace in its later years gets a closer look than a new heat pump, and a dusty, high-pollen neighborhood means condenser coils foul faster. We build spring cooling and fall heating visits around your equipment and climate, so every tune-up helps keep the system efficient and protects the equipment you've already paid for.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a regular maintenance schedule reduces operating costs. A clean coil transfers heat far more efficiently, a properly charged system stops overworking the compressor, and a fresh filter protects the blower motor. Most families find that a maintenance plan costs far less than replacing an HVAC system even one year early.

Proper Sizing

Equipment Sized for Your Actual House

When it comes to HVAC, bigger is not better. An oversized unit turns on and off too often, burns extra energy, and never runs long enough to pull humidity out of the air, while an undersized one runs nonstop and still can't keep up on the hottest days. We don't simply copy the tonnage of the old unit. Instead, we run a real load calculation that factors in your square footage, insulation, windows, and ductwork, and for mini-splits we size each head to the room it serves.

Houses change over time. A finished basement, an addition, or two new bedrooms can outgrow the single-stage system that once kept up just fine. Modern two-stage and variable-speed heat pumps hold tighter temperatures and use far less energy. Whether you're comparing a heat pump and a furnace-and-AC pair, or ducted and ductless, we'll lay out the honest pros and cons in SEER2 ratings, operating cost, electrical and venting needs, and installation work.

Common Problems

Heating and Cooling Problems We Solve in Olathe

Warm air from the vents, a furnace that refuses to light, a frozen evaporator coil, leaking refrigerant: each of these problems has a different cause and needs its own repair. Olathe HVAC tracks down the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's a condenser that hums without starting, a short-cycling furnace, a thermostat that loses its settings, a clogged condensate drain, or a mini-split flashing error codes.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A weak capacitor simply keeps the compressor from starting, but a cracked heat exchanger can leak combustion gases into your living space, and a blocked condensate drain can quietly ruin a ceiling. We match the fix to the failure: capacitors, contactors, and fan motors on cooling systems, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards on furnaces, sealed and repaired ductwork where conditioned air is bleeding into the attic, and refrigerant recovered and recharged after a repaired leak. Whatever you're facing, our team has the training and parts to handle it.
